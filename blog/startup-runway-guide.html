Startup Runway Guide – How Much Time You Have

Runway is how many months your company can operate at the current burn rate before running out of cash. It is one of the most important numbers for founders and investors. This guide explains how to calculate it, when to worry, and how to extend it.

What Is Runway?

Runway is the length of time (usually in months) you can keep operating at your current spending rate with the cash you have. It answers: “If nothing changes, when do we run out of money?”

Runway is critical for planning: when to fundraise, when to cut burn, and when to push for profitability. Investors often ask for runway and expect at least 12–18 months after a round so you have time to hit milestones before the next raise.

The Runway Formula

Runway (months) = Available cash ÷ Monthly burn rate

Available cash is the cash (and sometimes near-cash) you have on the balance sheet. Monthly burn rate is how much cash you spend per month (revenue minus expenses, or if you prefer, total operating expenses minus revenue when you are not yet profitable). Use consistent definitions: e.g. only operating cash, excluding credit lines you do not want to rely on for runway.

Gross Burn vs Net Burn

Gross burn is total cash spent per month (all expenses). Net burn is cash spent minus cash coming in (e.g. revenue, other income). For early-stage startups with little revenue, gross and net burn are similar. As MRR grows, net burn drops; eventually you reach cash-flow positive and runway becomes less urgent. When reporting runway, clarify whether you use gross or net burn—investors often assume net burn.

When to Extend Runway

Extend runway when: you are below 12–18 months and not yet ready to fundraise, market conditions are bad for raising, or you want to reach profitability without another round. You extend runway by: increasing revenue (faster growth in MRR), reducing expenses (cut burn), or adding cash (fundraise, bridge, or non-dilutive financing).

Cutting burn

Common levers: reduce headcount or replace with lower-cost options, cut non-essential tools and marketing spend, renegotiate contracts, and focus the team on fewer priorities. The goal is to lower monthly burn without killing growth or morale.

Runway and Fundraising

Investors want to see enough runway to hit the next set of milestones (e.g. next round metrics, or profitability). Raising when you have 6 months left is possible but stressful; raising with 12+ months gives you leverage. Use runway in your narrative: “We have 14 months of runway and plan to use this round to reach 24 months and achieve X.”

Common Mistakes

Using revenue that is not yet collected (e.g. annual contracts paid monthly); ignoring one-time expenses or seasonal spikes; mixing currencies or time periods; and overcounting cash (e.g. including restricted or committed funds). Be conservative: use actual cash and realistic burn so you are not surprised.
