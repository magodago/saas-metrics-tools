How to Calculate LTV – Customer Lifetime Value

Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) is the total revenue you expect from a customer over their entire relationship with you. This guide shows you how to calculate LTV and use it with CAC for better decisions.

What Is LTV?

LTV (Lifetime Value) is the total revenue (or gross profit) you expect from one customer over the full time they remain a customer. For SaaS, that usually means the sum of all subscription payments (and sometimes add-ons) until they churn. LTV answers: “How much is one customer worth to us over their lifetime?”

LTV is essential for unit economics. You want LTV to be meaningfully higher than your CAC—often at least 3:1—so that each acquisition is profitable after accounting for serving that customer.

The LTV Formula

A widely used formula for LTV is:

LTV = Average value per purchase × Purchase frequency (per year) × Customer lifetime (years)

For subscription SaaS, “average value per purchase” is often the average revenue per user per period (e.g. monthly ARPU). “Purchase frequency” is how many times they pay per year (e.g. 12 for monthly). “Customer lifetime” is the average number of years a customer stays. So LTV = ARPU × 12 × lifetime in years, or equivalently LTV = ARPU × (lifetime in months).

Another common approach for subscription businesses is LTV = ARPU ÷ Churn rate (with churn in decimal form, e.g. 0.02 for 2%). That gives you LTV in the same time unit as your churn (e.g. monthly churn → LTV in months). Both approaches can be valid depending on whether you prefer to model by lifetime or by churn.

Step-by-Step: How to Calculate LTV

Step 1: Define average value per purchase

For SaaS, use average revenue per user per billing period (ARPU). If you have multiple plans, you can use a revenue-weighted average. For non-subscription, use the average order value (AOV) that a typical customer pays per transaction.

Step 2: Define purchase frequency

How many times per year does the customer pay? For monthly subscription, that is 12; for annual, 1. You can also work in months: LTV = ARPU × average lifetime in months.

Step 3: Estimate customer lifetime

Customer lifetime is how long, on average, a customer stays before churning. You can derive it from your churn rate: average lifetime ≈ 1 ÷ churn rate (e.g. 2% monthly churn → 1/0.02 = 50 months). Use historical data when possible.

Step 4: Multiply

LTV = Average value × Frequency × Lifetime (in consistent units). Use our free LTV calculator: enter average value per purchase, purchases per year, and customer lifetime in years to get LTV per customer.

Why LTV Matters

LTV tells you how much you can afford to spend to acquire a customer (CAC) and still be profitable. It also guides pricing and packaging: if LTV is low, you may need to reduce CAC, increase price, or improve retention. Many investors and operators look at the LTV:CAC ratio and payback period (how many months of revenue it takes to recover CAC).

LTV and growth

Higher LTV means each new customer is more valuable. That can justify higher CAC in channels that bring better-fit customers, or it can allow you to invest more in acquisition while staying unit-economic positive.

Improving LTV

You can increase LTV by: raising prices or moving customers to higher tiers, reducing churn, increasing purchase frequency (e.g. annual plans with add-ons), and expanding revenue per account (upsells, cross-sells). Even small improvements in retention or ARPU compound into much higher LTV over time.

Common Mistakes

Using revenue instead of gross margin when your goal is profitability; ignoring churn or using unrealistic lifetime assumptions; mixing time periods (e.g. monthly ARPU with annual lifetime without converting); and not segmenting (enterprise vs SMB can have very different LTV). Use consistent definitions and, when possible, segment by cohort or plan.
