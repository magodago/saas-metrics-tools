What are SaaS Metrics?

SaaS metrics are the key numbers that show how healthy and scalable your subscription business is. Founders and investors use them to track growth, efficiency, and risk. The most important are CAC, LTV, MRR, churn, and runway.

What is CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)?

CAC is the total cost to acquire one new customer. You add up all marketing and sales spend in a period (ads, salaries, tools) and divide by the number of new customers. A lower CAC with the same quality of customer means more efficient growth. SaaS benchmarks often aim for CAC that is recovered within 12–18 months from the customer's LTV.

What is LTV (Lifetime Value)?

LTV is the total revenue you expect from one customer over their entire relationship with you. For SaaS it is often approximated as: average revenue per user per month × gross margin % ÷ monthly churn rate. LTV should be meaningfully higher than CAC (e.g. 3x or more) for a sustainable unit economy.

What is MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue)?

MRR is the predictable revenue your business earns every month from subscriptions. You sum the monthly value of all active subscriptions (e.g. if someone pays yearly, divide by 12). MRR is the backbone of SaaS forecasting and valuation. Growth in MRR and low churn drive company value.

What is Churn?

Churn is the rate at which you lose customers or revenue. Customer churn = customers lost in a period ÷ customers at the start × 100%. Revenue churn is similar but based on revenue lost. High churn hurts LTV and growth. Reducing churn is often the highest-leverage improvement for early-stage SaaS.

What is Runway?

Runway is how many months your company can run at the current burn rate before running out of cash. Runway = cash in bank ÷ monthly burn rate. Startups usually aim for 18–24 months of runway to have room to iterate. Short runway forces rushed decisions or down rounds.

Benefits of Tracking These Metrics

Tracking CAC, LTV, MRR, churn, and runway lets you see where to invest (e.g. channels with lower CAC), where to fix (e.g. high churn), and how much time you have (runway). Investors and boards expect these numbers. Using free calculators like ours keeps your basics accurate without extra cost.

Common Mistakes

Mistakes include: ignoring churn when projecting LTV, mixing one-off and recurring revenue in MRR, or counting only ad spend in CAC while forgetting salaries and tools. Another error is measuring CAC and LTV over different time windows. Use consistent periods and definitions so your ratios (e.g. LTV:CAC) are meaningful.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good CAC for SaaS?

It depends on LTV and payback. Often CAC is acceptable if you recover it in under 18 months and LTV is at least 3x CAC. Enterprise SaaS can have higher CAC; SMB should be lower.

How do I calculate LTV for subscription?

LTV ≈ ARPU × gross margin % ÷ churn rate. For monthly: LTV = (ARPU × margin) / monthly churn. Our LTV calculator does this for you.

What is a good churn rate?

Monthly churn under 2–3% is often healthy for SMB SaaS. Enterprise usually targets under 1%. Net revenue churn can be negative if expansion revenue outweighs lost accounts.

How much runway should a startup have?

Most aim for 18–24 months. Less than 12 months is risky unless you have a clear path to revenue or funding.
